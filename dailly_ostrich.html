Man Who Opened Meeting With 'Quick Question' Has Now Been Talking for 11 Days

Coworkers at a Lower Pemberwick paperclip distributor say they have stopped expecting an end, and have started rotating chairs in shifts.

MF
By Marjorie Thistlewood-FableSenior Correspondent, Meetings Desk · Updated 7:49 a.m. CT
PSF⧉

LOWER PEMBERWICK — What began as a routine 9:00 a.m. stand-up meeting at Brindlewick Fastener Supply entered its eleventh day Tuesday, after regional logistics coordinator Doug Plimsby-Harrow raised his hand and said, according to multiple witnesses, "Real quick question before we wrap."

He has not stopped speaking since.

"It started as a question about the Q3 paperclip forecast," said accounts associate Brenda Quillfeather, 44, who was reached by phone from her chair, where she has remained for 263 consecutive hours. "Then it became a question about Q4. Then about the concept of quarters. Around day six he got into whether a paperclip is technically a tool or a lifestyle."

Company leadership confirmed the meeting is ongoing and described morale as "stable, in the way a hostage situation is stable."

"I keep thinking he's about to land it. He makes the landing face. And then he says, 'Which actually brings me to another thing.'"— Gary Wobblesworth, intern, Day 9

A meeting that refused to end

Calendar records reviewed by The Daily Ostrich show the meeting was originally scheduled for 15 minutes. It has since been automatically extended 1,056 times by the company's scheduling software, which on Thursday began sending attendees a notification reading simply, "Are you okay?"

Facilities staff have adapted. Sandwiches are now delivered through the ceiling tiles by a small drone named Kevin. A cot was wheeled in on Day 4 but was declined by Mr. Plimsby-Harrow, who reportedly said he "didn't want to lose momentum."

Experts say the phenomenon is not unheard of. "We call it Unbounded Clarification Syndrome," said Dr. Penelope Gristlethorn, a professor of Meeting Studies at the entirely fictional Hollowmere Institute of Business Things. "In most cases, the words 'quick question' are a sincere attempt at brevity. In roughly one case in four million, they are a portal."

"He's a good guy," coworkers insist

Despite the duration, colleagues described Mr. Plimsby-Harrow as warm, well-meaning and "honestly kind of making some good points around day eight, before the part about his cousin's boat."

At least two attendees have attempted to leave. Both reported that each time they stood, he turned to them and said, "This actually affects you, so," and they sat back down.

The company's HR department has issued a statement encouraging staff to "hold all remaining questions until after the current question," and has quietly updated the employee handbook to define a quick question as "any question under 90 seconds, or roughly one-tenth of a sneeze."

As of press time, Mr. Plimsby-Harrow had paused briefly to sip water, prompting a wave of hope across the room, before clearing his throat and saying, "Okay, last thing."

Correction: An earlier version of this article said the meeting was in its tenth day. It is in its eleventh. We apologize to the attendees, who would like that day back.
